International Medical Standards & Guidelines

WombCare's AI reasoning is grounded in peer-reviewed guidelines from six leading international obstetric and radiology bodies.

Governing Bodies

Six International Authorities

WombCare's clinical thresholds, screening protocols, and report language are drawn from these bodies.

ISUOG

International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics & Gynecology

Primary source for fetal cardiac screening protocol, anomaly scan structure, and biometric measurement standards.

AIUM

American Institute of Ultrasound in Medicine

First-trimester exam guidelines, equipment standards, and obstetric ultrasound safety recommendations.

SMFM

Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine

Nuchal translucency thresholds, soft marker management, and high-risk pregnancy monitoring protocols.

ACR

American College of Radiology

Ultrasound appropriateness criteria, reporting standards, and radiation-free fetal imaging guidance.

ACOG

American College of Obstetricians & Gynecologists

Practice bulletins on gestational dating, PUL management, and obstetric ultrasound indications.

RCOG

Royal College of Obstetricians & Gynaecologists

UK ectopic pregnancy management, early pregnancy loss guidelines, and fetal anomaly screening program standards.

Trimester-by-Trimester Screening

What WombCare evaluates at each stage of pregnancy — from first-trimester viability to third-trimester growth surveillance.

First Trimester · 5–13 weeks

Viability & Dating

  • Gestational sac (MSD)
  • Crown-Rump Length (CRL)
  • Nuchal Translucency (NT)
  • Cardiac activity detection
  • Yolk sac morphology
  • Adnexal assessment
NT Scan · 11–13+6 weeks

Combined Screening

  • NT measurement (target <3.5mm)
  • Nasal bone assessment
  • Ductus venosus waveform
  • Tricuspid regurgitation
  • PAPP-A & free β-hCG
  • Down syndrome risk calculation
Second Trimester · 18–22 weeks

Anomaly Survey

  • ISUOG 5-plane cardiac screen
  • 3-axial neurosonogram
  • Facial profile & lips
  • Spine, abdominal wall
  • Limbs & genitalia
  • Biometry: BPD, HC, AC, FL
Third Trimester · 28–40 weeks

Growth & Wellbeing

  • Fetal biometry & growth centile
  • Umbilical artery Doppler
  • Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI)
  • Single Deepest Pocket (SDP)
  • Biophysical Profile (BPP)
  • Placental localisation
ISUOG Cardiac Protocol

The 5-Plane Cardiac Screening Sequence

ISUOG's recommended fetal cardiac screening protocol for the 18–22 week anomaly scan.

1

Four-Chamber View (4CV)

The foundational cardiac plane — detects ~50% of structural heart defects

Cardiac Axis

Heart should point to the left at 45° ± 20° from midline. Levocardia confirmed; dextrocardia flagged.

Chamber Symmetry

Left and right atria and ventricles should be roughly equal in size. Disproportion may indicate outflow obstruction.

Interventricular Septum

Assessed for continuity from apex to crux. VSD is the commonest congenital heart defect.

2

Left Ventricular Outflow Tract (LVOT)

Aorta arising from the left ventricle

The aortic root should arise from the posterior left ventricle, continuous with the interventricular septum. Subaortic VSD and aortic override (as in TOF) are identified here.

3

Right Ventricular Outflow Tract (RVOT)

Pulmonary artery arising from the right ventricle

The pulmonary artery should cross the aorta at ~90°. Abnormal relationship suggests transposition of the great arteries (TGA) — the commonest cyanotic heart defect detected post-natally.

4

Three-Vessel View (3VV)

Alignment of pulmonary artery, aorta, and SVC

PA, Ao, and SVC should form a V-shape with PA largest, Ao slightly smaller, and SVC smallest. Equal or reversed sizes suggest abnormality such as pulmonary stenosis or aortic coarctation.

5

Three-Vessel & Trachea View (3VTV)

Ductal and aortic arch convergence on the trachea

Both arches should converge onto the left side of the trachea forming a V-shape. A right aortic arch is associated with conotruncal defects and DiGeorge syndrome. The trachea should be midline.
